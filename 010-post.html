Byte Broadcast is coming back from the dead

The short version

I started a podcast called Byte Broadcast three years ago. Then I stopped. There was no dramatic ending — no falling out, no burnout post, no final episode where I explained myself. It just quietly stopped being the thing I did on Tuesdays, and then it had been a year, and then it had been longer than that.

It's coming back. This is the coming-soon post, which means I'm publishing the intention before I've published the episode, on purpose, because that's the only version of this that has ever worked for me.

Why it died

I want to be accurate about this instead of flattering, because the reason it died is the same reason most solo dev projects die and it's worth naming.

Consulting pays on Friday. A podcast pays in maybe. When a client week goes sideways — and client weeks go sideways constantly — the thing that gets cut is never the billable thing. It's always the one with no invoice attached. I didn't decide to quit. I made the same small rational trade about forty times in a row, and forty small rational trades add up to a dead podcast.

The second reason is less flattering. The original pitch was the intersection of web development, AI and crypto, decoded into byte-sized insights. That was a topic list, not a show. Topic lists are exhausting to host, because every episode starts from nothing and you're performing an interest in a category rather than reporting from inside something. I ran out of things to say about a category. I have not run out of things to say about my actual week.

What's different this time

Three years later that same triple — web, AI, crypto — stopped being a trend list and turned into my job description. I run Profullstack, which means I'm consulting on web work, building payment infrastructure that settles in crypto, and doing most of the actual development with agents at 2am. The category caught up with the calendar.

So the show has a subject now, and the subject is the work:

  • Consulting for real. Not “how to find clients.” How scope actually moves, what a bad-fit client looks like six weeks before you admit it, what you charge and why that number keeps being wrong, and the specific feeling of a pipeline that is either empty or on fire and never in between.
  • Solo development. One person maintaining an unreasonable number of products, which is only possible now and is possible in a way that is stranger than the demos suggest. What agentic coding is genuinely good at, where it quietly costs you more than it saves, and what shipping looks like when code review is a thing you do to yourself.
  • The twists. The parts that get edited out of the founder recap: the product that had users and no business, the pivot you resisted for a year and were right to resist for eleven months of it, the month where the money worked and the month right after where it didn't. Entrepreneurship narrated forward is mostly wrong. I'd rather narrate it from inside, while I still don't know how it turns out.

What it won't be

No guest-of-the-week grind. Guests when there's a reason, not to fill a slot. No numbered-tips episodes. No pretending any of this was a plan, because the honest through-line of the last three years is a sequence of things I tried, most of which taught me something and some of which just cost money.

And I'm not promising weekly. Weekly is what I promised last time, and the promise is what killed it — once you're behind on a cadence, every episode you make is late, and late feels bad enough that you make fewer of them. It ships when there's something worth an hour of your attention. That's the whole commitment.

Why announce before there's an episode

Because I already know how the quiet version goes. I have run that experiment. A revival nobody was told about is indistinguishable from not reviving it, and it gives me a free exit any week I'd rather bill hours instead.

So: this is the announcement. It's on the record, it's in my feed, and now the version of me who wants to skip it in three weeks has to walk past this post to do it.

bytebroadcast.com/podcast — subscribe there and the first one lands in your inbox. If you want a specific question answered on the show, about consulting rates or the agent workflow or what a bad year actually looks like from the inside, send it. I would much rather answer something real than open with an introduction.

See you soon-ish. Which is exactly as much as I'm willing to promise.
