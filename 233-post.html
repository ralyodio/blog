w3bs.org has accounts now. One account works on the site, in the w3bs command line, and in any app that adds a "Sign in with W3BS" button.

Getting in

Make an account at w3bs.org/signup. You pick a username and give an email address. We send a link, and the account exists once you click it. That way nobody can register your address before you do, and the form never tells a stranger whether an address already has an account.

After that you sign in with a passkey or a password. A passkey is the better choice: your fingerprint, face or device PIN, and nothing a phishing page can steal. The emailed link stays around as the fallback for a lost password or a new laptop.

The command line

curl -fsSL https://w3bs.org/install.sh | sh
w3bs login
w3bs whoami

w3bs login opens your browser, you approve it once, and the CLI keeps its tokens in ~/.config/w3bs/auth.json. w3bs logout revokes the session on the server too, not just on your machine. Every signed-in device is listed on your account page, where you can sign any of them out.

For app developers

w3bs.org is an OAuth 2.1 authorization server. Register an app on your account page and you get a client id. There is no client secret: every app uses the authorization code flow with PKCE, which works the same for web, mobile and native apps.

Refresh tokens rotate on every use. If an old one ever shows up again, the whole sign-in is revoked, because two parties holding the same token means one of them stole it. The metadata is at /.well-known/oauth-authorization-server and the details are in the developer guide.

The code is open source in profullstack/w3bs.org.
