The job board is a blackhole

2026-09-06, by Anthony “chovy” Ettinger.

How this was written: drafted with an AI assistant from my own notes, then edited by me.

I think I unlocked a badge.

For about fifteen years I applied to jobs the way everyone does. Find the posting, fill the form, upload the resume the form then asks you to retype by hand, click submit. Nothing happens. Not a rejection, nothing. The application went into a queue that a human opens only if a filter lets it through, and the filter was tuned by someone who has never done the job.

That is not a hiring process. It is a way of not reading applications at scale.

The thing that actually works has always been the same. Someone inside the company walks over to the hiring manager, or sends one Slack message, or forwards an email, and says you should talk to this person. That skips the queue completely. Everybody knows this. The reason people still use the form is that the referral requires knowing who to ask, and most of us do not.

So that is the part I pointed the machine at.

The question is narrow and it is a search problem. At a company I do not work at, who is close enough to the hiring team that their word carries, and close enough to me that they will answer at all? Same employer at some point. Same stack. Same open source project. Same city. A mutual connection who would vouch. That is a lot of profiles to read, and reading a lot of things and finding the overlap is the one thing these models are genuinely good at.

So Claude Code does the reading. It goes through LinkedIn, builds the list, and tells me who the actual path is. Then it drafts the message, and I send it through myna from the terminal like everything else I ship.

The message matters more than the volume. I am not asking a stranger to refer me, which is a thing nobody can say yes to. I am asking a question they can answer in one sentence. Who owns this team. Would you be willing to forward this. That is a small favor, and people say yes to small favors from people who have something in common with them.

I am running this right now for a role in San Francisco. Agentic coder, up to 500k a year. Which is a genuinely strange sentence to type, because the job is being good at the thing I have been doing at my kitchen table for months.

No outcome yet. I am not going to write the post where I claim a system works before it has worked. What I can tell you is what it feels like from this side, which is completely different. The form is a slot machine. This is a conversation with a person who has a reason to reply.

The other thing I noticed is that it is the same pipeline as everything else I have built this year. Find the people who care, work out the actual path to them, say something specific, send it from a CLI. I built that for getting software in front of users. It turns out it is also just how you get in front of a hiring team.

The job board never needed to be a blackhole. It is one because sending a thousand applications is cheap and reading them is not. Fine. Then stop sending applications.
