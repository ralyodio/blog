It started about seven minutes ago

As I write this it is 10:37 in the morning in New York, the opening ceremonies kicked off at 10:30, and the seventeenth Hackers On Planet Earth conference is underway at the New Yorker Hotel on 34th and Eighth, one block from Penn Station. It runs Friday through Sunday, August 14–16, roughly 10am to 11pm each day, and then keeps going into the small hours.

You do not need a ticket to watch. All three tracks are being webcast free:

  • Track 1 — Grand Ballroom, Day 1 — the main stage stream
  • hope.net — the conference site, with per-room stream links
  • schedule.hope.net — the full grid

The stream is run by ISOC Live, Joly MacFie's operation out of the Internet Society's New York chapter, which has been quietly webcasting HOPE for years. “Internet history, as it happens” is their tagline and it is not an exaggeration — a lot of what you can watch this weekend will be cited later.

What HOPE actually is

HOPE has been run by 2600: The Hacker Quarterly since 1994, when it was the first large-scale hacker conference attempted in the United States, inspired by the Dutch outdoor gatherings of the 80s and 90s. It ran every two years for fifteen editions, went annual in 2025, and is now organized under a 501(c)(3) called HOPE On Planet Earth.

The keynote list over the years reads like a history of the argument about what computers are for: Steve Wozniak, Edward Snowden, Chelsea Manning, Cory Doctorow. The recurring themes are dissent, censorship, and whistleblowing, and the crowd is deliberately not a corporate security crowd. HOPE is all-ages, and it bills itself as being for “hackers, makers, tinkerers, experimenters, artists, educators” — which in practice means a talk on homomorphic encryption is scheduled two rooms away from a paper-popup village and a lockpicking table.

If you have only ever been to a conference where the badge has a sponsor's logo on it and the hallway track is recruiters, this is a different thing entirely.

Why the venue is the story

HOPE was born at the Hotel Pennsylvania and lived there, on and off, for most of its life. The last Manhattan HOPE was in 2018. Then the Hotel Penn was demolished — the building that gave the world PEnnsylvania 6-5000, gone — and the conference moved out to St. John's University in Queens for 2022 through 2025. Per HOPE's own history page, that ended with them being asked to leave.

So this is the first HOPE in Manhattan in eight years, in a building that had to be found from scratch. The New Yorker went up in 1930, has over a thousand rooms, and is where Nikola Tesla lived out his last years and died. The organizers noticed. There is a talk on the schedule tonight called Nikola Tesla's Time at the New Yorker Hotel, in the Crystal Ballroom, at 5pm Eastern.

The other detail from the announcement that made me laugh: the attendee hotel rate includes unlimited domestic and international phone calls from the room. At a conference founded by the magazine that grew out of phone phreaking. In the hotel where Tesla died. I am sure nothing interesting will come of that.

Day one, if you are watching the stream

All times Eastern. The Grand Ballroom is the Track 1 stream linked above.

  • 10:30 — Opening Ceremonies (Grand)
  • 11:00 — Cameras, Cops, and Cell Towers: A Field Guide to Modern Surveillance, Michael Raymond (Grand)
  • 1:00pm — From Source to Story: Cryptography and Psychosocial Care Protects Public Interest Journalism, Jen Helsby and Harlo Holmes (Grand)
  • 2:00pm — Leaking and Investigating the Epstein Files, a panel with Emma Best, Mikael Thalen, Alexander J. Urbelis, Raphael Satter and Kyrie (Grand)
  • 3:00pm — Privacy's Defender: My 30 Year Fight Against Digital Surveillance, Cindy Cohn of the EFF (Grand)
  • 5:00pm — Rapid Response Tech, on tech infrastructure in the Twin Cities anti-ICE resistance (Grand)
  • 6:00pm — Hackers: Journalism Needs Your Help, Brandon Roberts of ProPublica (Grand)
  • 7:00pm — Inside North Korea's Underground Tech Resistance, Jon Thompson (Grand)
  • 8:00pm — How Advertising Libraries in Mobile Apps Enable a Massive Location Surveillance Apparatus, William Budington and Lena Cohen (Crystal)
  • 10:00pm — Please Wiggle Responsibly — A Dance Extravaganza (Grand), because it is still a hacker con

Saturday has Ask EFF, 2026 Edition at noon, Remembering Hackers 31 Years Later at 1pm with Renoly Santiago and Emmanuel Goldstein — the 1995 movie is old enough to have its own retrospective panel now, which is a sentence I did not enjoy typing — and How to Be a Whistleblower at 7pm with Emma Best, John Williams and Jeremy Hammond. Sunday opens with BlueLeaks 2.0 at noon and closes at 6pm.

The through-line across all three days is hard to miss. Surveillance advertising, facial recognition evasion, Signal's actual threat model, stalkerware and domestic violence, mesh networks and off-grid radio, de-Googling your organization, running your website over the radio to beat censorship, and an entire cluster of talks on what AI agents do when nobody is watching them. This is a conference about staying legible to each other and illegible to everyone else.

A small thing I appreciate

The schedule is served over curl. Not as an API you have to read docs for — as an actual terminal-rendered grid:

curl https://schedule.hope.net/hope26/schedule/
curl https://schedule.hope.net/hope26/schedule/?format=list

It draws the room columns in box characters and bolds the talk titles with ANSI escapes. There is also a real schedule app on F-Droid, not just Google Play, which tells you something about who they expect to show up.

That is the whole ethos in one detail. The conference that ran the first big hacker gathering in America still ships its program in a form you can pipe into grep, and streams every room for free to anyone who could not get to 34th Street.
