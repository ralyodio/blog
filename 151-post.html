Apple’s Real Competition Is the Tab You Already Have Open

Apple's most revealing launch metric might come from a website that had nothing to do with the launch.

During the September 9 Apple event, Pornhub reported a sharp fall in pageviews from Apple devices. The biggest dip coincided with the iPhone Duo reveal. A foldable phone apparently earned a temporary interruption in some people's regular browsing.

According to Mashable's reporting, reproduced by CNCB News, Apple-device pageviews were already 19.1 percent below their usual level two minutes before the presentation began. During the Duo announcement, the decline approached 22 percent. Pornhub told Mashable that was its largest drop during a September Apple event in five years.

Android traffic stayed much closer to normal. Even during the foldable reveal, the reported decline on non-Apple devices was about five percent.

That split is the interesting part. Apple was much better at interrupting the people already using its products.

Apple introduced the iPhone Duo as its first foldable iPhone. A visibly different device gives people a reason to watch a demonstration. You can understand the change before anyone has finished explaining the processor.

For people building products, there's a useful question here: what would make someone stop what they're doing to look at yours?

Most launches compete with an existing habit. The audience has a video playing, a group chat open, a job to finish or somewhere else it would rather be. Getting the announcement onto a screen is only the beginning. Something about it has to justify switching attention.

Still, these numbers deserve a little restraint. A decline in pageviews does not identify where people went. It doesn't prove they watched Apple's stream, and it certainly doesn't tell us whether they will buy a phone. Pornhub supplied the figures, and the timing makes the connection plausible; this isn't a controlled experiment.

The attention didn't last, either. By the end of the event, Apple-device pageviews were reportedly only about seven percent below normal.

I would treat that as a reminder to show the interesting part of a product early. A launch can win a few minutes of curiosity. The product still has to give people a reason to come back after the presentation is over.
