Paying people to seed: pay2seed, paid2seed, pay2stream, paid2stream

2026-09-06, by Anthony “chovy” Ettinger.

How this was written: drafted with an AI assistant from my own notes, then edited by me.

Piracy is the best distribution network ever built. BitTorrent moves more bytes for less money than anything a cloud vendor sells. It has two problems, and they are the same two it had in 2004. Nobody is paid to seed, so a swarm lives as long as somebody feels like leaving a client open. And nothing about an upload says who put it there or whether they were allowed to, so every seeder is presumed to be up to something.

Fifteen years ago I ran torula.com, one of the first IPTV reseller platforms. Around then a Russian team shipped Ace Stream, a BitTorrent engine that carried live television to millions of people at once. They never opened it and never paid a relay. I have wanted to do that properly ever since. This week we published four specs that do.

The idea

Consent on the way in, payment on the way out.

Consent is an attestation. Before a hub lists anything, the person adding it signs a record saying what it is, why they may distribute it (their own work, a licence that allows it, an open licence, public domain, or their own private backup), and where to send a notice. A false one is voided on notice and counts against a public standing. That record is the difference between an IPTV reseller and a pirate, and a machine can now check it.

Payment is a market. Someone puts money at a hub for a swarm to be kept alive by N seeders for M days, or for a live channel to be carried by N relays for a window. Seeders and relays take leases, prove every period that they hold and serve the bytes, and are paid per GiB-month or per relay-hour. Proofs are storage challenges answered from disk, and probes where a verifier connects as an ordinary peer and pulls pieces. That works on a plain public torrent, which is what makes seeding open data payable.

The naming is the rule. pay2* is the client protocol, the side that pays over HTTPS and plays. paid2* is the server protocol, the BitTorrent side that earns. pay2seed and paid2seed cover files, archives, backups and feeds. pay2stream and paid2stream cover live channels.

HLS out of the box

The live half has a gateway role: a relay that also speaks HTTP. It turns the swarm into standard HLS, clear or AES-128 sealed, and serves the M3U plus XMLTV pair every IPTV app asks for on setup. VLC, TiviMate, Kodi, an Apple TV or a smart TV plays a paid, consented, peer-carried channel with nothing installed. The viewer's ticket is a pass bound to the gateway, which signs the per-byte vouchers on their behalf up to the ticket's cap.

What we are building it with

Nothing new. The specs sit on things we already run.

  • OpenSwarm, our spec family for paid encrypted swarms: ipfile, iplive, ippay for passes and vouchers, ipdb for a catalogue on the DHT.
  • x402 and CoinPay for the money. An offer is bought the way a pass is: a 402 with a USDC quote, a signed proof, done.
  • bittorrented.com as the reference hub. Its seedbox rental and IPTV resale rails are the central-server version of this already.
  • torlink as the reference seeder client. We added per-torrent seed time upstream this week so a client can pin a lease's end date, and it runs on our seed boxes now.
  • c0mpute nodes as paid storage and gateways. A c0mpute pin job and a pay2seed offer are the same request.
  • logicsrc for the specs, at logicsrc.com/openswarm, with a PRD alongside.

Drafting was done with Claude Code in a background session, which read the family, wrote the documents, split them when I said client and server should be separate, and opened the pull requests. I reviewed the ideas and kept the names.
