The $875 proxy bill was a 1.5 MB manifest

2026-09-04, by Anthony “chovy” Ettinger.

How this was written: drafted with an AI assistant from the day's commits, session logs and my notes, then edited by me.

Yesterday ran about 21 hours across a dozen repos. Most of it was shipping audio rails. The part worth writing down is the bill.

The bill

Late in the evening a proxy invoice came in at $875. We share one rotating residential proxy across projects and barely use it, so the obvious theory was a leaked credential being drained by someone else. I asked the assistant to sweep every repo for it.

The sweep found something bad, just not the thing I was looking for. One credential, hard-coded in several repos, sitting in two public ones on GitHub, with secret scanning switched off on both and never rotated since it was issued. That got fixed: rotated by hand in the vendor dashboard, moved to an environment variable everywhere, gated behind a flag that defaults to off, and every one of our 193 vaults swept for stale copies.

But nobody was stealing it. The consumer was our own SiriusXM player on bittorrented.com. Every audio segment was going through the residential proxy, even though a docstring in the same file said segments must skip it to save budget. Residential bandwidth is billed per gigabyte. Worse than the segments was the manifest. SiriusXM's media playlist is 1.5 MB, listing 1,845 segments, and the player re-polls it every few seconds. The playlist file was costing roughly ten times the audio.

WhatPer listener hour
64 kbps audioabout 29 MB
1.5 MB manifest, six polls a minuteabout 540 MB

The fix is a budget file: per-user and global caps on bytes, requests and concurrency, answered with 429 and a Retry-After. The proxied fetch helper now refuses any call made outside the SiriusXM session context, so the next "temporary" use has to be deliberate. The residential line was already returning 402 Payment Required, so the app moved to the datacenter plan. Our own env example claimed SiriusXM blocks datacenter IPs. It does not, or at least it did not yesterday. Token refresh, browse, tune, key and segments all came back 200.

SiriusXM on tipoffwatch

Earlier in the day the same rail landed on tipoffwatch.com, so a game page can play the radio call from your own SiriusXM account. Three versions went out. The login bug that ate the middle of the night had three causes and none of them was SiriusXM. A Promise.all destructure was one slot off, so everyone rendered as connected. The team-to-station table held ESPN's bare league ids where it wanted sport-prefixed slugs. And every call was 402ing because of the proxy above. There is now a test that refuses a session lookup inside any Promise.all.

Also: the radio bundle was 900 KB because the bundler inlined the player's optional engines. Marking mpegts.js external and swapping hls.js for its light build brought it to 400 KB, 127 KB gzipped.

Spotify on bittorrented.com

It started with "does Spotify have a listen API?" It does not, for playback. It ended as media-streamer 0.4.0: you pair your own Premium account, the server runs one librespot process for you, it shows up as a Spotify Connect device on your phone, and the audio is restreamed as HLS through our player.

Things that cost real time. The device-auth flag is not in librespot's 0.8.0 tag, so the image builds a pinned dev commit and checks its hash. Password login has been gone since 2024. The pairing prompt prints on stdout, so audio has to leave through the subprocess backend instead.

The last bug of the day: paired, playing, and every song skipped after a few seconds. librespot's subprocess backend has no clock. It writes PCM as fast as the pipe accepts, ffmpeg swallowed a whole track in about zero seconds, librespot reported end of track, and Spotify advanced. Measured in a container, ten seconds of PCM took 0 seconds without ffmpeg's -re flag and 9 with it.

The rest

tsbb, the BBS behind bbs.hqtui.com, went from 0.2 to 0.5 and now updates itself from GitHub releases every five minutes. cli-tools got three releases, including a web-pentest toolbelt that installs the Kali tools on plain Ubuntu. moshcode 0.91 keeps one SSH connection open per workspace. And three of the fifteen answer engines in an AEO audit flagged yesterday's date as an invalid future date, because their clocks are their training cutoffs. I did not backdate anything.

Read the manifest size before you proxy it.
