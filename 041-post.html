Red Bull took my eye

Three weeks ago I wrote a post about my left eye being shut and said I would keep the medical side to myself. I have had time to think about it, and I want to write down how I got here. The title is unfair to Red Bull. I will get to that.

The sequence

A while back a blood test put me in the pre-diabetic range. I heard it and did nothing about it. No change to what I ate, no change to how much I moved, no follow-up. That is on me, and it is the actual cause of everything that follows.

Then I added a Red Bull every day. Not as a treat. As the thing I reached for when the day got long, which with the amount I ship is every day. That went on for about six months.

The next blood work said type 2 diabetes. Not pre. The full thing.

Within that same stretch the vision in my left eye went out of focus one morning, and by evening the lid had drooped closed. The diagnosis was a third cranial nerve palsy. The third nerve runs the muscle that lifts the eyelid and most of the muscles that point the eye. When it stops working, the lid drops and the eye drifts out, and you get double vision until it drops all the way, at which point you get one eye.

The most common cause of a third nerve palsy in an adult my age is diabetes. High blood sugar damages the tiny vessels that feed the nerve, the nerve loses its supply, and it shuts off. The scans in the ER were to rule out an aneurysm, because that is the other thing that does this. I did not have an aneurysm. I had sugar.

The straw

Red Bull did not do this to me. Years of not taking a warning seriously did this to me. The can was the last straw on a camel that I had been loading for a long time. A standard 8.4 ounce Red Bull has 27 grams of sugar, and the 12 ounce can has 38. For a 20 year old with a working pancreas that is a sugary drink. For a 50 year old who has already been told he is pre-diabetic it is a daily dose of exactly the wrong input, and I was the one who knew that and drank it anyway.

I thought of the habit as caffeine and the diagnosis as sugar, and never put them in the same sentence. The caffeine is a rounding error. What I learned is simple: Red Bull is for 20 year olds. It is not for 50 year olds, and it is definitely not for a 50 year old with my blood work.

Now

No Red Bull. No energy drinks. The diabetes is being treated, which is how the palsy gets treated: the nerve recovers on its own once the sugar comes down, typically over a couple of months. Mine is not back yet. The lid is still down, I still miss doorknobs, and video calls still look like I am winking at you.

The advice from the earlier post stands: sudden vision or eyelid symptoms are an ER question. I have one more piece now, and it is aimed at me as much as at you. A pre-diabetic result is not information. It is a deadline. I had one and I let it pass. Look at what you drink every day, read the label, and act on the warning while it is still a warning.
