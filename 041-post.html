Red Bull took my eye

Three weeks ago I wrote a post about my left eye being shut and said I would keep the medical side to myself. I have had time to think about it, and the medical side has a cause worth naming. It is in a can.

The sequence

A while back a blood test put me in the pre-diabetic range. I heard it, I did not do anything about it. Then I started drinking Red Bull every day. Not as a treat. As the thing I reached for when the day got long, which with the amount I ship is every day. That went on for about six months.

The next blood work said type 2 diabetes. Not pre. The full thing.

Within that same stretch the vision in my left eye went out of focus one morning, and by evening the lid had drooped closed. The diagnosis was a third cranial nerve palsy. The third nerve runs the muscle that lifts the eyelid and most of the muscles that point the eye. When it stops working, the lid drops and the eye drifts out, and you get double vision until it drops all the way, at which point you get one eye.

The most common cause of a third nerve palsy in an adult my age is diabetes. High blood sugar damages the tiny vessels that feed the nerve, the nerve loses its supply, and it shuts off. The scans in the ER were to rule out an aneurysm, because that is the other thing that does this. I did not have an aneurysm. I had sugar.

What is in the can

A standard 8.4 ounce Red Bull has 27 grams of sugar. The 12 ounce can is 38. If you are already pre-diabetic, a daily dose of that is exactly the wrong input. I knew energy drinks were not health food. I did not connect a habit I thought of as caffeine with a diagnosis I thought of as sugar. They are the same thing. The caffeine is a rounding error next to what the sugar does to someone whose pancreas is already losing.

I cannot prove the Red Bull did it. Nobody can run the control. But the order is pre-diabetic, six months of daily Red Bull, type 2, nerve palsy, and I do not think it takes a doctor to draw the line through those points.

Now

No Red Bull. No energy drinks. The diabetes is being treated, which is how the palsy gets treated: the nerve recovers on its own once the sugar comes down, typically over a couple of months. Mine is not back yet. The lid is still down, I still miss doorknobs, and video calls still look like I am winking at you.

The advice from the earlier post stands: sudden vision or eyelid symptoms are an ER question. I have one more piece now. If a blood test says pre-diabetic, that is not information. It is a deadline. Look at what you drink every day, and read the label, because the sugar you are not counting is the sugar that counts.
