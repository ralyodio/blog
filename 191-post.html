I had about 250 GB of photos and videos in folders called Camera, Downloads and New folder (3). Search was hopeless because nothing in there had words attached to it. So I built MediaAnalyzer.

Point it at a folder. It writes a short, searchable description next to every photo, video, song and document, and moves each one into the folder it belongs in, from a list you control. PDFs, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OpenDocument, e-books, Markdown, camera RAW, Photoshop files, MP3 and FLAC all work: anything ffmpeg, ImageMagick, LibreOffice or pandoc can open.

curl -fsSL https://mediaanalyzer.pro/install.sh | sh
mediaanalyzer login
mediaanalyzer scan ~/Pictures
mediaanalyzer apply ~/Pictures --yes

Originals stay home

The CLI shrinks each photo to a 768 pixel preview on your machine and uploads only that. Videos become a single 3×3 contact sheet of frames, so a two hour video costs the same as one photo. Documents become a picture of the first page and a short text excerpt; songs become their tags and cover art. The server re-encodes every upload, which strips EXIF and GPS, and deletes the preview as soon as the file is described. We keep the text, never the pictures.

Nothing moves until you say so

apply prints the plan first: how many files land in each folder, and the first moves. Nothing changes until you add --yes. Name clashes get (2) instead of overwriting anything, every move is journaled, and mediaanalyzer undo puts the tree back exactly as it was. Re-running scan resumes where it stopped and never pays for a file twice, even after apply has moved it.

Bring your own model, no typing

The Providers page connects OpenRouter in one click. For Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, Groq or Together, a button opens the page where you make a key; paste it and it is checked, saved, and a good vision model is picked for you, with the cost per 1,000 photos shown next to each one. A self-hosted Ollama or vLLM works too.

On your own key MediaAnalyzer costs $0.20 per 1,000 files. A cheap vision model such as Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite adds about $0.25 per 1,000 photos on your provider's bill, so a 40,000 photo library comes to roughly $18 all in. Hosted tiers on our own GPUs are coming.

mediaanalyzer bench runs the same sample through a self-hosted vLLM model and Claude side by side and writes a page of their descriptions next to each other, so you can judge by eye whether the cheap model is good enough before you spend anything.

Also in DiskPush

DiskPush 0.12 grew a plugin system, and MediaAnalyzer is the first plugin: select files in the desktop app or the TUI, choose Analyze media, and the descriptions and folders appear right there.
