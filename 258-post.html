deadman.switch: so your crypto doesn’t die with you

Most people who hold crypto have the same quiet problem. The coins are safe because only they know where the keys are. That is also exactly why the coins are gone the day something happens to them. The family knows there was "some Bitcoin". Nobody knows there is a Ledger in the safe, or that the seed is split between a bank box and Mom's house, or which exchange the rest is on.

So I built deadman.switch.

How it works

  1. Seal it. Write down what your people would need: which wallets, where the hardware is, how the seed is stored, the exchange logins, who to call. Add files if you like. It is encrypted in your browser (PBKDF2 into AES-256-GCM) with a passphrase you choose. We store the locked box, never the key.
  2. Give them the passphrase. In your will, on paper in the safe, in person. You can leave a hint that only they will understand.
  3. Check in. Every week, month or year we ask "still here?" by email and on your phone. One tap, and the clock starts again.
  4. If you stop. Three reminders spread across a grace period you pick, then, on the paid plan, a phone call. Only after all that, and at least a day after the last reminder, does each person you chose get their own link. They type the passphrase, and the bundle opens in their browser.

Before it fires they have the key but no box. After it fires we have the box but still no key. There is nothing on our side for anyone to leak.

The details I cared about

  • Missing a check-in never fires anything at once. A server outage can't skip the warnings either: reminders are at least 12 hours apart, and the release waits a full day after the last one.
  • Check-in links need a tap, so a mail scanner that opens links can't check in for you.
  • If a paid plan lapses, your switches keep running. Protection never switches off because a renewal was missed.
  • Going off-grid? Pause it.

For the terminal and for agents

bunx @profullstack/deadman status
bunx @profullstack/deadman checkin
bunx @profullstack/deadman seal "My crypto" note.md --hint="first dog, then the year"
claude mcp add deadman -- bunx @profullstack/deadman mcp

There is a JSON API too. The MCP server can check in and manage switches, but sealing stays in your browser or the CLI, so an agent never touches the passphrase.
