What a backlink costs

When we priced backlinks for our own sites, the going rate came back somewhere between $445 and $4,090 per link. That is for one link on one site. Nobody at our size is paying that, so for a long time we just did without and watched the SEO advice tell us we were leaving money on the table.

The usual advice for a small SaaS is the same list every time. Google Ads, LinkedIn Ads, Meta. Techdella has a decent writeup of it if you want the full version. It is fine advice. It also assumes you have a budget, and most of the projects I run do not.

What we do instead

We use two things on CrawlProof, and both are free while they are in beta. No card.

The link exchange. You get matched with other verified sites and you trade links. The part that makes it not a link farm is the ledger: every exchange is recorded, both sides are verified, and you can see what you gave and what you got. It is the same outcome as the $445 link, minus the $445.

The ad network. Two sides, and we are on both.

As an advertiser you paste a landing page URL and it designs the banners for you, on brand, from whatever is already on the page. You edit the copy and colours or upload your own. Then you set a daily budget. Clicks get attributed with your own ref tag, and bot and duplicate clicks are filtered before billing, which matters more than it sounds like it does.

As a publisher you flip a project on as an ad slot, drop one script tag, and pick a payout coin. Earnings accrue per click and withdraw on chain to your wallet through CoinPay. No fiat, no invoicing anybody, no minimum payout threshold that quietly eats the balance.

Three things that bit us

Running this on real sites turned up a few things that are not in any docs, so here they are.

  • New ad slots are created inactive. The serve endpoint answers {"ok":false} for every format until you activate the slot, which looks exactly like a broken embed. Check the slot status before you debug anything else.
  • The script scans the page once. It fills every ad div at DOMContentLoaded and then stops. Anything that mounts later, tabs, infinite scroll, any React content behind a fetch, has to call window.crawlproofAds.scan() itself.
  • Do not render a desktop and a mobile banner and hide one with CSS. Both fill. The hidden one burns an impression that no human ever saw. Pick one by viewport and render that.

Worth it?

For us, yes, mostly because the alternative was nothing. We were never going to buy a $1,300 link. We were never going to fund a Google Ads campaign for a side project. Trading links with sites in the same position, and running ads that settle in crypto with no card on file, is a version of this that actually fits how we work.

It is in beta and it is free, so the worst case is you waste an afternoon. crawlproof.com.
