Cards and crypto in one checkout

2026-08-23, by Anthony “chovy” Ettinger.

How this was written: drafted with an AI assistant from my own notes, then edited by me.

I wanted to know how much of a payments integration is left over once you can just ask for it. So I asked for one: cards and crypto, same checkout. What follows is what actually ran, including the part where it did not work.

One call, two rails

CoinPay's create endpoint takes a payment_method of crypto, card, or both.

$ curl -s -X POST https://coinpayportal.com/api/payments/create \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
    -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
    -d '{
      "business_id": "41ba69c3-...",
      "amount_usd": 25.00,
      "currency": "usdc_pol",
      "payment_method": "both"
    }'

That comes back with a freshly derived Polygon address and a live Stripe Checkout session, from the same request:

{
  "payment_address": "0x7f57E618d4C0dF60c1d5299BFAc9126A76384f1D",
  "crypto_amount": "25.01250125",
  "metadata": {
    "stripe_checkout_url": "https://checkout.stripe.com/c/pay/cs_live_..."
  }
}

$25.00 became 25.01250125 USDC because the one cent network fee rides on the customer side rather than coming out of the settlement. The address is good for 15 minutes.

The customer sees one page

Send them to /pay/{payment_id} and they get tabs: pay with crypto, or pay with card. The card tab hands off to Stripe.

The useful part is behind that. Both rails fire the same payment.confirmed webhook, at the same URL, with the same signature header. metadata.payment_rail tells you which one it was. Your fulfillment code has one path, not two, and the branch (if you even want one) is a field read.

What still needs a human

Cards run through Stripe Connect, so each business does Express onboarding once:

$ coinpay card connect onboard <business-id> --country US --email me@example.com

That prints a link to a hosted Stripe form asking for bank details and a tax ID, and no CLI is going to fill that in for you. The country is locked at account creation, so get it right the first time. The crypto side needs none of it; funds forward straight to your wallet.

Fees: 0.5% on crypto. On cards the platform fee goes through Stripe's application_fee_amount, 0.5% on Pro and 1.0% on Free, on top of whatever Stripe takes.

The part where it did not work

I wired all that up, loaded the checkout in an actual browser, and got the crypto panel with no card tab. Not a fluke of my account either. Every dual rail checkout, for every merchant.

The page decides whether to offer a card tab by reading payment.metadata.stripe_checkout_url off the public GET /api/payments/{id}. That endpoint selects an allowlist of columns, and metadata was not on it. So the URL never arrived and the tab never rendered. The markup had been shipping in the bundle the whole time with nothing able to reach it.

The fix is one column, with a catch. Returning metadata as it sits also publishes the fee breakdown, the wallet source, redirect targets and, on some rows, a user id, to anybody holding a payment id. So it selects the column and projects exactly one field back out of it.

Watching the card rail in real time

Cards are where the fraud actually lands, so every card checkout gets screened before the Stripe session exists. The screen returns allow, verify or block. Verify forces 3-D Secure, which moves stolen card liability to the issuer. Block returns a 403 and no session is ever created. It fails open on purpose, because a bug in screening should not stop payments.

Every decision is logged, and declines and disputes are written back from the Stripe webhook. Ten days in:

kind             decision   count   score
checkout_screen  allow        133      10
checkout_screen  verify         1      35
card_declined                  27
dispute                         6

The diagnosis is more interesting than the total. There are 16 rules and exactly two have ever fired. The single verify was three charges under $5 inside ten minutes with no buyer email attached, 25 points plus 10, landing exactly on the verify threshold. That is card testing, and it got stepped up to 3-D Secure.

The other rule is the problem. no-email fired on 134 screens out of 134. Nothing passes a buyer email through the create call, so every checkout carries the same 10 points and the score only starts to mean anything once a velocity rule fires off history. A signal present 100% of the time is not a signal.

The bad traffic is concentrated. One operator accounts for 27 of the 33 decline and dispute events. A second runs two businesses under two different webhook domains, tied together by a shared merchant id. That is why the linkage rules match on shared webhook host and shared wallet rather than on email: the pattern is rotation, so burn one account and open another behind the same billing panel. Checking any single account in isolation misses it.

What does not exist yet is the diagnosis surface. Everything above is a SQL query I ran by hand. The logging is real, the dashboard is not.

The integration really was one sentence. Finding out that the thing I asked for did not render took loading it in a browser, which is still the only way anyone finds that out.
