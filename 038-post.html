A one-field funnel for launch.anapp.now

2026-09-03, by Anthony “chovy” Ettinger.

How this was written: drafted with an AI assistant from my own notes, then edited by me.

back to my blog postings

launch.anapp.now went live today. It is the campaign site for Chovy, and it has one job: get a visitor to type an app idea and carry that sentence into Chovy without making them type it twice. Here is how the funnel is built, and what I left out on purpose.

One field, no signup form

There is no email capture. The call to action is a textarea with the placeholder "I want an app that..." and a button that says Launch this idea. Six chips (Marketplace, SaaS, Booking app, Customer portal, Mobile app, Internal tool) drop in a starting sentence for anyone who freezes at a blank box. The same form repeats at the bottom of the page, because whoever scrolled that far has read the pitch and should not have to scroll back up.

An email address is a promise to talk later. An idea is the first step of using the product. Asking for the idea filters for intent better than any form field does, and it gives Chovy something to do the second the person lands.

The handoff

Submitting posts the idea to the microsite's own API, which checks the length, a honeypot field, and a limit of six submissions per ten minutes per address. The API then calls Chovy server to server with a shared secret and gets back a one time handoff URL. The visitor sees a short "Opening your starting point in Chovy" screen and lands in the signup dialog with the idea already filled in.

The idea text never appears in a URL, a cookie, a log line or an analytics event. That was a rule from the first commit. People type things they have not told anyone yet, and a query string ends up in referrer logs and browser history.

The price lives on Chovy, not on the campaign page: $2,499 once per app, card or crypto, paid before the first build. The microsite sells starting. The pricing page sells the build.

Four headlines

The hero is an A/B test with four variants at 25 percent each: the control "Launch an app. Now.", a shorter cut of the same, "Ship the app idea. It's been in your head long enough." and "Have the idea. Skip the coding career." Assignment happens at the edge and sits in a 90 day cookie, so a returning visitor sees the headline they saw before. The weights are read from an environment variable on every request, so moving traffic toward a winner is a variable change, not a deploy. Adding ?hero= and a variant name forces one, which is how I check layouts.

Tracking without pixels

No Google tag, no Meta pixel, no PostHog. Eleven events (landing view, variant assigned, input focused, typing started, submitted, handoff started, completed or failed, and a few more) are batched and sent with sendBeacon to the site's own endpoint, which forwards them to Chovy. Chovy adds what only it can see: handoff opened, signup completed, plan generated, project started.

UTM parameters, the referrer and the landing path are stored as first touch in a cookie and attached again as last touch on every event. The report is on Chovy and breaks the funnel down by headline, source and device. The number I watch is project rate: of the people who typed an idea, how many started a project.

Copy angles

The page talks to the product person, not the coder. "You already do the product part." Ownership comes up again and again: your code, your domain, your decisions, your team. A simulated morning, 9:12 to 12:02, shows what a first session looks like rather than describing it. The FAQ deflates its own headline. Does "now" mean my app is instantly live? No.

No countdown, no discount, no fake scarcity. The only urgency on the page is the true kind: the idea has been sitting in your head for a while.

What is not done

An honest list. There is no email follow up, so someone who types an idea and abandons the signup is gone. No paid channels are running; the tracking is built for them, but nothing is buying traffic yet. The hero images are placeholders from a script, because the image API I meant to use is out of credit. And there are no numbers to share, because the site has been up for a day. I will write again when there are.

The site is open source at github.com/profullstack/launch.anapp.now, if you want to see the handoff and the event batching for yourself.
