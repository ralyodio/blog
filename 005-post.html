My left eye is closed for a while

2026-08-16, by Anthony “chovy” Ettinger.

How this was written: drafted with an AI assistant from my own notes, then edited by me. It is deliberately light on details.

The short version

Ten days ago my vision went out of focus without warning, and over the course of a day my left eyelid drooped further and further until it was effectively shut. That got me a trip to the emergency room and a night in the hospital for scans and observation.

The upshot: it is a nerve problem affecting the muscles that move that eyelid and eye. It is a known thing, it has a name, and it is being treated. The scans did not turn up the worst-case answers. I am home, I am fine, and I am under the care of people who do this for a living.

The expected timeline I was given is on the order of a couple of months for it to resolve, with follow-ups along the way. That is all I am going to say about the medical side. I am writing this because a shut eye is hard to miss on a video call, and I would rather post once than explain it forty times.

What it is actually like

Stranger than I expected, and less dramatic than it looks.

The lid itself is the obvious part — it sits closed, and lifting it is not something I can just decide to do. The part nobody warns you about is depth perception. With one eye you do not lose the world, you lose the distance to it. I have missed a doorknob. I have set a glass down about an inch to the left of where the counter was. Stairs require a moment of deliberate thought that stairs have never previously required.

Reading is the thing that surprised me most, in a good way. One working eye reads fine. Screens are fine. The fatigue is real by evening, but the workday holds.

What this means for work

Not much, honestly, and that is the point of this section.

  • I am working. Same projects, same clients, same commits. If anything I have been at the keyboard more, because the keyboard does not require depth perception.
  • Fonts got bigger. Everything is a couple of steps up in size, and I have stopped pretending I can work from a laptop panel across a room.
  • Video calls will look odd for a while. I am not squinting at you, I am not unimpressed, and I am not winking. Just the one eye for now.
  • I am driving less. This is the one real constraint. If we had plans that involved me behind the wheel, let's revisit them.

Agentic tooling turns out to be a genuinely good fit for a week like this one. A lot of what I do already looks like describing what I want and reviewing what came back, and reviewing text is about the most one-eye-friendly task there is.

The unsolicited part

Two sudden symptoms and I still spent an hour talking myself out of going in. Do not do that. Anything sudden involving vision, pupils, or an eyelid is an emergency-room question, not a wait-and-see-how-it-looks-tomorrow question. The scan that rules something out is worth the evening, and the one that finds something is worth considerably more than that.

Everyone involved — the ER staff, the specialist who came by the next day, my family — was excellent, and I got clear answers within about twenty-four hours. That is a better outcome than a lot of people get, and I know it.

So

Normal posting continues. If you see me around with one eye shut, now you know, and you do not have to ask. If you were about to ask anyway: I am fine, it should sort itself out, and no, I cannot wink on purpose — that would require the other one.
